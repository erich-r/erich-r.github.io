## The idea

At around May of this year (2026), I decided that walking 100.000 steps in a single day would be a good idea.

I made the decision on a Thursday (30th of April), with the challenge scheduled for Saturday (2nd of May). That left me with approximately three days to prepare, which is not exactly what you would call a proper training plan.

My preparation consisted of roughly one week of walking around 10.000 steps a day. Not much, but apparently enough for me to convince myself that walking ten times that distance was reasonable.

On Friday, I went grocery shopping and bought supplies for the challenge, which indludes:

- 1 pack of smoked salmon
- 3-4 slices of emmental cheese
- 2 bananas
- 500 ml of Coca Cola
- 500 ml of Monster (no sugar)
- 6 slices of bread
- Electrolytes
- 1 cheese pizza
- 2 protein bars

I made 3 sandwiches, one with the smoked salmon and the others with the cheese.

In order not to go into hunger crisis I planned to eat something every hour, keep walking, and somehow reach 100.000 steps before the day ended (basically I took the three sandwiches I made and divided them into halves). 

## 4:30 AM - Breakfast of champions

My day started at 4:30 AM.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/blog/100000-steps/pizza_breakfast.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Breakfast of champions" style="max-width: 350px;"><figcaption>White pizza, eaten rigorously cold</figcaption>
</figure>

Before even beginning the challenge, I ate the entire cheese pizza. I figured that starting with a full stomach was probably better than discovering halfway through the day that I had run out of energy. I also thought that the pizza was a good choice due to the high presence of carbs and the light-to-moderate presence of protein from the cheese.

At 5:00 AM, I started walking away from Trento, heading south towards Rovereto.

The streets were quiet, the sun had not yet risen, and I had absolutely no idea how much my feet were going to hate me later.

## 5:00–10:00 AM - Everything is going great

The first few hours went surprisingly well.

I ate something every hour, I also drank water every 15 minutes more or less, and maintained a pace of approximately 7.000 steps per hour, which meant that after five hours I had accumulated around 35.000 steps.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/blog/100000-steps/half_sandwich.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Half Sandwich" style="max-width: 350px;"><figcaption>One of my hourly snacks</figcaption>
</figure>

I reached Rovereto around 10:00 AM feeling reasonably good. At that point, the challenge seemed much more manageable than I had expected.

I stopped at the Bici Grill for a proper breakfast: a pistachio brioche and a cappuccino with sugar.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/blog/100000-steps/second_breakfast.png' | relative_url }}" alt="breakfast at a bar" style="max-width: 350px;">
</figure>

After about 15 minutes of rest, I continued south, walking almost reaching Mori before turning around and heading back towards Trento.

So far, so good.

I still had food, I had drinks, and my legs were still cooperating. The challenge was progressing nicely.

I was also about to discover that walking 35.000 steps and walking 80.000 steps are two very different experiences.

## 12:00-PM 5:00 PM - The return journey

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/blog/100000-steps/1217status.png' | relative_url }}" alt="where I was at 12:17 PM" style="max-width: 350px;">
  <figcaption>My position at approximately 12:17 PM</figcaption>
</figure>

Leaving Rovereto and heading back toward Trento was when things started to change.

My pace gradually dropped, and I began feeling the first aches in my feet. Nothing unbearable yet, but enough to remind me that this was going to be a long day. I also did the fatal mistake to look at the number of steps done so far, to see how much I still needed to walk: as soon as I saw only 45.000 steps I started to worry considerably.

I drank the Monster, hoping the caffeine would give me a boost. Almost zero to no effect (I never use energy drinks, so I was hoping to get a boost, but that was not the case).

Then I switched to the Coca Cola, which seemed to help more. Whether it was the sugar, the caffeine, or simply the psychological effect of drinking something different, I felt that I had a little more energy.

My pace was now somewhere between 4.000 and 5.000 steps per hour, with more frequent breaks.

The walking itself was becoming less enjoyable, but I was still moving forward. At this stage, the main challenge was to keep walking without resting too much in order to keep my feet functional.

By the time I reached Trento again, I had accumulated approximately 70.000 / 75.000 steps.

It was around 5:00 PM.

I stopped at some benches just outside of Trento, where I started taking more frequent breaks. I removed my shoes whenever I could, letting my feet breathe and hoping that a little rest would make the next stretch easier.

Unfortunately, the pain was becoming deeper and more persistent.

Every time I started walking again, I could feel it. The first few minutes after a break were particularly unpleasant, and it was getting harder to ignore what my feet were telling me.

Still, I had reached 80.000 steps. Only 20.000 remained.

A friend joined me at this point to keep me company for the final part of the challenge.

Having someone to walk with helped me a bit after several hours being alone. The remaining distance was still considerable, but at least I was no longer doing it alone.

We continued walking, with breaks roughly every 20 minutes. Each break helped temporarily, but getting started again was painful. Now the pace dropped to around 2.000 steps per hour, Feelsbadman.

At this point, I wasn't really walking for fun anymore, but I was too close to stop.

## 5:00–8:00 PM - The final 20.000 steps

At around 5:00 PM, I stopped in a grocery store and bought a Powerade for a final boost of energy.

By around 8:00 PM, I had reached 90.000 steps.

My friend had to leave, so I was on my own again.

Ten thousand steps remained.

At the beginning of the day, 10.000 steps had seemed like an ordinary walk. Now, they felt like a separate challenge entirely.

I decided to do one final loop around the city. My pace wasn't particularly impressive, and the frequent breaks continued, but I kept going.

Eventually, I reached 99.000 steps.

At that point, I decided that a little reward was appropriate. I stopped at a gelateria and ordered a two-scoop gelato.

Then I continued toward the bus stop.

Finally, at approximately 9:45–10:00 PM, I reached 100.000 steps!

## The aftermath

After getting home, I checked my feet, expecting to find blisters after such a long day of walking.

Surprisingly, there weren't any.

I had worn two pairs of socks to reduce friction, and apparently that simple decision had helped considerably.

My feet were still sore, of course, but avoiding blisters after 100.000 steps felt like a small victory of its own.

Looking back, the challenge was a mixture of endurance, stubbornness, food, caffeine, and increasingly questionable decisions.

If I could change somethings, I would have brought some sunscreen for sure because the sun was scorching; I also would have started the walking with a more reduced pace, because I think I started the challenge too much strong.

The preparation was minimal, the nutrition strategy was improvised, and the pain during the final hours was much worse than I had anticipated.

But I completed it.

Physically I don't consider myself an athletic person at all, but I knew this challenge was 90% mental rather than physical, so I guess that anybody can complete it with the right attitude.

However, don't ever underestimate the importance of eating and drinking every once in a while: if you don't and you start feeling hungry, it's already too late.

Would I recommend doing it with only a week of 10.000 step days as preparation? Probably not.

Would I do it again?

Maybe if I have company, yes.

## Some numbers

Of course, walking 100.000 steps in one day raises another question: how much energy did I burn, and how much did I eat?

I can only make a rough estimate...

### Calories burned

Walking 100.000 steps likely corresponds to somewhere around 80 km (I did 79 km), depending on stride length.

For someone weighing 85 kg, that's a substantial amount of physical activity.

My estimated energy expenditure looks something like this:

| Activity | Estimated calories |
| --- | --- |
| Basal metabolism | 1.800–2.000 kcal |
| Walking 100.000 steps | 3.500–5.000 kcal |
| Other additional expenditure | 200–500 kcal |
| Estimated total | 5.500–7.500 kcal |



### Calories consumed

I didn't track everything precisely, but the estimation would be:

## Final thoughts

The first 35.000 steps felt manageable. The next 45.000 were progressively harder. The final 20.000 were mostly about patience and persistence.

In the end, I walked from Trento toward Rovereto, continued almost to Mori, returned to Trento, circled the city 2-3 times, and finished with a two-scoop gelato.

Approximately 100.000 steps, around 17 hours between starting and finishing, and somewhere in the neighborhood of 7.000 calories expended.

Not the most efficient way to spend a Saturday, perhaps.

In a not so distant future I'll try to convince some of my friends to join me for a 100.000 Steps Challenge: Part 2 - Electric Boogaloo.
